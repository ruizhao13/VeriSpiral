# Model and human contributions

VeriSpiral was developed with AI assistance under human direction. Generated
material is not treated as correct or accepted merely because it appears in the
repository.

## Human authority and responsibility

The user owns the accepted:

- scientific model and assumptions;
- research target, comparison class, and success or kill conditions;
- verifier contract and the meaning permitted for each status; and
- boundary of scientific judgment, including correctness, novelty,
  paper-worthiness, and manuscript-facing claims.

For a model or verifier revision, only the user may `accept`, `reject`, `modify`,
or `branch`. The project owner separately controls repository releases, privacy,
attribution, and operational policy. One person may hold both roles, but the
permissions remain distinct from AI assistance.

The project owner is responsible for reviewing public claims, source
extractions, third-party attribution, and this contribution account before
release.

## AI assistance

AI may assist research by searching and organizing literature, proposing
solution candidates, developing proof routes, seeking counterexamples,
designing experiments, and preparing model- or verifier-revision discussion
packets. These are intended responsibilities in the architecture; the public
runner does not execute those live research activities.

AI models also assisted development of this repository by drafting and revising
documentation, schemas, prompts, code, tests, fixture narratives, and release
checks. That assistance can speed up work but does not establish truth, novelty,
ownership, authorship, or scientific acceptance.

The checked-in theory-certificate fields are AI-assisted public-demo inputs.
The runner checks registered fields and arithmetic; it does not validate the
paper extraction or reproduce the proof. A project-owner source review remains
necessary before publication.

## Decision and feedback fixtures

The five-stage candidate demo replays a repository-registered executable
verifier and binds the resulting receipt to the complete semantic subject.
Human acceptance remains pending, so its decision is `await_human_acceptance`
and it emits no success trace or Skill. A receipt pass is not a user decision or
general scientific validation.

The process-feedback demo consumes one synthetic event and emits an unapplied
process patch proposed for human review. Its source is bound to registered
executable control evidence. It does not capture live feedback, apply the patch,
or learn a user's preferences over time.

The research-specification demo validates a pre-registered event whose actor is
`human` and binds it to a discussion and specification hash. An accepted
verifier-only revision becomes an immutable successor on the same target
lineage; a model revision creates a separate lineage whose work cannot solve the
original target. The label is fixture data: the repository neither captures the
decision live nor verifies the real-world identity of its author. A recorded
user decision controls permitted specification state; it does not prove a
scientific claim.

## Evidence boundary

Primary-source records, executable results, independent recomputation, domain
review, and explicit user decisions have different roles. Provenance or a
mechanical gate can show where an input came from and whether declared checks
passed. Neither turns model self-consistency into independent verification.

## What is not claimed

- The project was created autonomously by AI.
- AI assistance is authorship or bears accountability.
- A user decision proves scientific truth.
- The runner performs live literature search, algorithm generation, theorem
  proving, personalization, long-term learning, or autonomous scientific
  judgment.
- This account determines legal ownership or academic authorship.
